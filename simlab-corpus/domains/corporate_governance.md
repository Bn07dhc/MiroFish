# Corporate Governance Simulation Patterns

## Deal Dynamics
Corporate deals (M&A, joint ventures, strategic investments) follow predictable phases:

### Phase 1: Assessment (Rounds 1-8)
- Information asymmetry is highest
- Only executives and deal team have full picture
- Board members ask probing questions
- Market speculation begins if any leak occurs
- Recommended agent activity: executives high, board moderate, market low

### Phase 2: Due Diligence (Rounds 8-18)
- Information gradually spreads to wider circle
- Operational teams start to feel impact
- Competitor intelligence activity increases
- Regulatory pre-engagement may begin
- Recommended: all agent types active, competitor and regulatory ramp up

### Phase 3: Negotiation (Rounds 18-25)
- Key terms being debated
- Board alignment becomes critical
- Market sentiment crystallizes
- Potential competing bids surface
- Recommended: executive and board highest, market reactive

### Phase 4: Decision/Close (Rounds 25-30)
- Final governance approval
- Market pricing reflects consensus
- Operational integration planning begins
- Recommended: board highest influence, executive execution-focused

## Stakeholder Dynamics in APAC
### Korean Corporate Culture
- Hierarchical decision-making (상명하복)
- Consensus-building (합의) before formal decisions
- Face-saving considerations in public communications
- Chaebol dynamics — cross-shareholding, related-party considerations
- Government relationship as quasi-stakeholder

### Singapore/APAC Hub
- Multi-jurisdictional complexity
- Regulatory arbitrage opportunities
- Cross-cultural communication challenges (Korean ↔ HQ)
- Singapore MAS/EDB as enablers (not just regulators)

## Data Center Industry Specifics
- Long-cycle assets (20-30 year useful life)
- Power procurement as key variable
- Zoning/permitting as gatekeeper
- Hyperscaler demand signals drive valuation
- Sustainability/ESG increasingly relevant
- Korea: KEPCO relationship critical
- Japan: NTT/KDDI ecosystem
- Australia: grid constraints, renewable mandates
